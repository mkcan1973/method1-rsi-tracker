All three lines normalized to $1,000/position, on the same calendar axis. Realized is a step function (flat between exits, jumps on each one); unrealized is the mark-to-market value of positions still open at each date (0 before a position's own entry date); total is their sum — today's rightmost point is your actual current P&L.
Historical year view: every trade is bucketed by its ENTRY year (like a fund's "vintage year" reporting), not by when it exited. A position still open at that year's own Dec 31 is marked closed at that date's price, not today's -- this view never changes once the year is over. The chart above shows REALIZED P&L only (no "unrealized" line, since nothing in a closed year is still open) -- built from the trade list itself, not daily price history, which is why a historical year's chart was possible at all without the live DB needing to carry years of price data just for this.

Strategy: buy when RSI(14) ≤ 20 (oversold), hold until RSI ≥ 65, flat otherwise — validated via walk-forward backtesting across the S&P 500 + core ETFs/index, then chosen over the original 30/55 textbook defaults via a grid search that replicated out-of-sample (see method1/threshold_grid.py and threshold_train_test.py). This page is a reporting view of that signal, not investment advice. Entry/exit prices use the next trading day's OPEN after the signal-crossing close, since a close that triggers RSI crossing a threshold isn't itself a tradeable price.

P/E and EPS @ entry are both approximations (P/E = entry price ÷ CURRENT trailing EPS; EPS is that same CURRENT trailing figure directly) -- neither is the EPS that was actually current on the entry date, since there's no free historical EPS series to compute it exactly. Reasonably accurate for trades entered in the last ~2 quarters and increasingly approximate for older ones, since EPS changes every earnings report. Trades with no reported EPS show as — for both and are excluded by either filter, not treated as having a P/E or EPS of zero.
